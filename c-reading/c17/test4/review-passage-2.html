Does education fuel economic growth?

A

... 9,000 handwritten inventories listing over a million personal possessions... Ogilvie... believes they may hold the answer to a conundrum that has long puzzled economists: the lack of evidence for a causal link between education and a country’s economic growth (Q15).

B

‘Education helps us to work more productively... but, if you look back through history, there’s no evidence that having a high literacy rate made a country industrialise earlier.’ During this period, Germany and Scandinavia had excellent literacy rates (Q23/24), but their economies grew slowly and they industrialised late. ‘Modern cross-country analyses have also struggled to find evidence that education causes economic growth, even though there is plenty of evidence that growth increases education (Q23/24),’ she adds.

C

From badger skins to Bibles, sewing machines to scarlet bodices – the villagers’ entire worldly goods are included (Q18). ... multiple indicators for the same individuals, making it possible to analyse links between literacy, numeracy, wealth, and industriousness... (Q15)

D

‘We can follow the same people – and their descendants (Q19) – across 300 years of educational and economic change,’ she says. Stories like that of the 24-year-olds Ana Regina and Magdalena Riethmüllerin, who were chastised in 1707 for reading books in church instead of listening to the sermon (Q20). ... The database also reveals the case of Juliana Schweickherdt... When Juliana continued taking jobs reserved for male guild members, she was summoned before the guild court and told to pay a fine (Q21) equivalent to one third of a servant’s annual wage. It was a small act of defiance by today’s standards (Q16)... The dominance of guilds not only prevented people from using their skills, but also held back even the simplest industrial innovation (Q22).

E

One way to look at whether education causes economic growth is to ‘hold wealth constant’ (Q14). This involves following the lives of different people with the same level of wealth... It is possible to discover whether education was, for example, linked to the cultivation of new crops, or to the adoption of industrial innovations like sewing machines. (Q14)

F

‘German-speaking central Europe is an excellent laboratory (Q17) for testing theories of economic growth,’ she explains. ... local guilds... were extremely powerful and legislated against anything that undermined their monopolies. In villages throughout the region, guilds blocked labour migration (Q25/26) and resisted changes that might reduce their influence (Q25/26).
